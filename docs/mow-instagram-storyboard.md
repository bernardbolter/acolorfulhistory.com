# Mediums of War — Instagram Storyboard
**WWI + WWII + Vietnam**

Format: 1 still + 4 video frames per post, square (1:1)
Each post opens on the full painting still, then 4 voiced video frames.
All frames voiced. Music chosen from Instagram library, runs under entire post.
Captions carry the content — voice is presence and personality.

---

## POST 1 — WWI in Black and White

**Frame order logic:** Photography establishes the concept early, human unawareness plants the thread, sky and color close with the Zeppelin double punchline.

---

### Frame 1 — STILL — Full Painting
**What:** The complete painting, clean and centered. No text overlay.
**Purpose:** Opening image. Let the monochrome do the work — pale painted sky above a dense grey mass below.
**Production:** Hi-res photograph of painting, color correction only.

---

### Frame 2 — VIDEO — Medium Reveal (long opener)
**Theme:** Medium / photography
**Movement:** Slow zoom on painted surface → reveal photograph in paint → crossfade to source photo → crossfade back to paint
**Script:**
*"In the war-torn trenches of World War One, there were brave individuals who didn't fight — but did something for all of us in the present day. They took photographs. These photographs help us today to see the horrors and chaos of what it's like when the world goes to war. But in black and white. They don't look like photographs from today, they look old and dated. But they look real as fuck."*
**Source material needed:** 1 source photograph — soldiers in action, nobody looking at camera. Credit required.

---

### Frame 3 — VIDEO — Human Unawareness (plants the thread)
**Theme:** Human interaction with the image
**Movement:** Slow pan across lower half → punch in on 2-3 figure clusters in trenches, nobody facing camera
**Script:**
*"In the trenches and the battlefields, nations faced off in bloody conflict. They were focused and engaged with the enemy. But ain't nobody looking at the camera."*
**Source material needed:** None — painted surface only.
**Note:** This line sits quietly until WWII Post 2 activates it. Don't over-explain.

---

### Frame 4 — VIDEO — The Sky
**Theme:** Horizon line / war moving into the sky
**Movement:** Wide shot → slow zoom up into pale sky → land on sparse biplanes and zeppelin
**Script:**
*"Above the battlefields of this great battle was only the sky, with just a few of the recently invented mechanical birds flying around. And a goddamn Zeppelin."*
**Source material needed:** None — painted sky only.

---

### Frame 5 — VIDEO — Color / Paint
**Theme:** Color / the painted surface
**Movement:** Slow drift across painted color zones → close on border where grey photograph meets soft painted field
**Script:**
*"The world of the 1910s was one of muted elegance. Though they were living on the cutting edge of technology for their time, they still didn't have the bright synthetic objects and loud streetwear fashion of our time. But they still had some big ass Zeppelins in the sky."*
**Source material needed:** None — painted surface only.
**Note:** Zeppelin callback from Frame 4 — gives the post a personality and makes people rewatch.

## POST 2 — WWII in Color

**Frame order logic:** Human awareness opens as immediate payoff from WWI's planted line — long frame, earns the runtime. Sky escalates the visual drama. Color/paint is the fourth wall break. Medium reveal closes on the concept and the title.

---

### Frame 1 — STILL — Full Painting
**What:** The complete painting, clean and centered. No text overlay.
**Purpose:** The color hits immediately after a day of grey. No words needed — the shift is visceral.
**Production:** Hi-res photograph of painting, color correction only.

---

### Frame 2 — VIDEO — Human Awareness (long opener, payoff of WWI thread)
**Theme:** Human interaction with the image
**Movement:** Slow pan across figure clusters → zoom into soldiers facing camera → hold on large portrait figure far left, looking directly out
**Script:**
*"Barely 30 years after the world tore itself apart, it was doing it again. The places had shifted, the players had changed only a little — but the technology had leaped forward. Not only was the world now being captured in color, but people remembered the images from the first war.*
*[pause]*
*And now they were looking at the cameras."*
**Source material needed:** None — painted surface only.
**Note:** This is the payoff of WWI's "ain't nobody looking at the camera." The zoom into the eyes of the large portrait figure on the far left as the last line lands.

---

### Frame 3 — VIDEO — The Sky
**Theme:** Horizon line / escalation
**Movement:** Wide → slow zoom up through crowded sky → drift through formations, parachutists → land on the blimp among the bombers
**Script:**
*"The soldiers fought in the trenches and cities as in the previous conflict, but now there was the thunderous roar of bombers and jets flying in formation in the sky above, fighting to the death.*
*But someone still showed up with a blimp."*
**Source material needed:** None — painted sky only.
**Note:** The blimp directly echoes WWI's zeppelin — last trace of the previous war floating among bombers.

---

### Frame 4 — VIDEO — Color / Paint (fourth wall break)
**Theme:** Color / the painted surface / artist's hand
**Movement:** Slow drift across vivid painted color fields
**Script:**
*"The world had changed to a place it could never come back from. The only thing left was for an artist, many years later, to paint this.*
*Oh — that's me.*
*The technology changed the way we see the past. So that's how I paint it."*
**Source material needed:** None — painted surface only.
**Note:** Only fourth wall break in WWII. One per series is enough. The pause on "oh — that's me" is everything — find it in the recording.

---

### Frame 5 — VIDEO — Medium Reveal (closer, title lands)
**Theme:** Medium / color photography / series title
**Movement:** Close on vivid painted color field → pull back to reveal color photograph in painting → crossfade to original source photo in full color → crossfade back to paint
**Script:**
*"A spiritual medium channels the dead. Makes the past present. Gives voice to those who can't speak anymore. And the medium is the message — for this medium of war."*
**Source material needed:** 1 source color photograph. Credit required.
**Note:** First time the series title is named explicitly across all three posts.

## POST 3 — Vietnam War on Video

**Frame order logic:** Sky carries the WWII thread and is the visual climax of the horizon line story across all three paintings — long frame, earns the runtime. Medium reveal bridges to the human close. Total occupation frame connects sky, camera and paint. Human unawareness closes the trilogy.

---

### Frame 1 — STILL — Full Painting
**What:** The complete painting, clean and centered. No text overlay.
**Purpose:** The sky dominates immediately — the horizon line has shifted dramatically down. The progression across three days is complete.
**Production:** Hi-res photograph of painting, color correction only.

---

### Frame 2 — VIDEO — The Sky (long, carries WWII thread)
**Theme:** Horizon line / total air dominance
**Movement:** Wide → slow zoom up through helicopter-filled sky → drift through scattered hovering helicopters → land on the compressed ground zone far below
**Script:**
*"After the war that was supposed to end all wars failed to do so, the skies only became more contested. A new machine emerged — one that didn't soar or dive, but hovered. Descended. Moved through the air in ways never seen before. The helicopter had arrived, and with it, a new kind of war from above.*
*But even with all that air power, the enemy just went underground.*
*And won."*
**Source material needed:** None — painted sky only.
**Note:** "And won." as its own beat. Same pause structure as "and now they were looking at the cameras" and "but someone still showed up with a blimp."

---

### Frame 3 — VIDEO — Medium Reveal
**Theme:** Medium / video / bridges to human close
**Movement:** Close on a video still on the canvas → still comes to life into actual footage → pull back to reveal it sitting in the painting → zoom into ground zone left side, the farming with machine guns detail
**Script:**
*"In the Vietnam War, another technology was introduced to the war story — the video camera. Recording the fighting, the flying, the running, the shooting, the dying.*
*And even some farming. With machine guns on the back."*
**Source material needed:** 1 source video clip. Credit required.
**Note:** "With machine guns on the back." lands as its own beat. The farming detail is bottom left of the painting — figures in the field with artillery mounted behind them.

---

### Frame 4 — VIDEO — Total Occupation
**Theme:** Sky, camera, news, paint — total occupation
**Movement:** Slow drift across the blue sky → zoom gradually into pure blue paint as last line lands
**Script:**
*"In all of recorded history, no conflict had ever so completely dominated the sky. The video camera, a technology still in its infancy, recorded everything it could see — and what it saw was relentless. The nightly news carried those images into every living room, every dinner table, every household in the nation. There was no escape from it. The war had occupied not just the sky, but the minds and hearts of an entire generation.*
*And the blue paint in this painting occupies most of the canvas."*
**Source material needed:** None — painted surface only.
**Note:** End on pure blue. The zoom into paint as the last line lands is the visual payoff.

---

### Frame 5 — VIDEO — Human Unawareness (trilogy close)
**Theme:** Human interaction with the image / full trilogy arc
**Movement:** Slow pan across the compressed ground zone figures — nobody facing the camera — then pull back to show the full painting one last time
**Script:**
*"The 20th century was filled with great technological advancements — and some of the most destructive and horrific events mankind has ever seen. This series has been about both. From the start of it all in World War One, when the camera was so new that people were just fighting, not realizing they were being captured for posterity. To the next world war, where every participant had seen the imagery from the previous one — and looked at the camera, and posed for the historians who would one day look back. But then we get to Vietnam. The most captured war the world had seen up until that point. They probably posed for the camera like their predecessors. But they couldn't hold that pose forever. And the video camera depicted the war again in all its realness.*
*And that's my story and I'm sticking to it."*
**Source material needed:** None — painted surface only.
**Note:** This is the long dismount. If someone has followed all three days they have earned it and they will want it. The pull back to the full painting at the end closes the visual arc. Step out of Attenborough on the last line — own it completely.
